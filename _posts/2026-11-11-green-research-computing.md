This post introduces the hidden environmental cost of using digital tools within research. The concept of green research computing is presented with the goal to carry out the same research at a lower environmental cost.

<!--more-->

# Why this matters

When thinking about research's carbon footprint, it can be easier to think of flights to international conferences or lab equipment. But every analysis we run, dataset we store and model we train also uses energy, water and hardware, often out of sight.

The ICT (Information and Communication Technology) sector is estimated to produce [2.1-3.9%](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2021.100340) of global greenhouse gas emissions, which is comparable to the [entire aviation industry](https://ourworldindata.org/global-aviation-emissions).

Computing is resource intensive in [various ways](https://www.software.ac.uk/guide/introduction-green-computing). Briefly, data centres' electricity use drives large emissions of greenhouse gases (GHGs); computing hardware requires rare-earth elements and minerals extracted using intensive mining; and e-waste is one of the fastest-growing streams of waste in the world.

This digital environmental impact is a hidden cost of research. Computing enables research, so the aim is to perform the same research at a lower environmental cost, not to do less research.

# Green Computing

> We define green computing as practices that minimise the negative impact of computing on the environment. - [Institute for Research Software](https://www.software.ac.uk/guide/introduction-green-computing)

There is a growing movement towards green computing. Researchers and professionals are developing and sharing methods and tools to reduce emissions. The goal is to make the digital side of research greener.

It starts with understanding where the impact is: from institutional choices like hardware procurement and e-waste, to everyday decisions such as storing large datasets no longer needed or how we request resources on High Performance Compute (HPC). Once we can measure and see the impact, we can start reducing it.

Green computing can start with small steps. Over time these steps, along with talking about them with colleagues, can lead to meaningful changes.

# Quick wins

Here are four things you can do now to lower your environmental impact:

- Use the [Green Algorithms calculator](https://calculator.green-algorithms.org/) to calculate the carbon footprint of your computations.
- Don't over-request HPC resources as this can waste resources. See [a recent talk](https://zenodo.org/records/22047068) presented by Dr Fred Sonnenwald ([Research IT](https://students.sheffield.ac.uk/it-services/research)) at [RSECon26](https://rsecon26.society-rse.org/) on this topic.
- Delete data you don't need in line with funder's and university's retention requirements. [Storing 1TB of data is around 10 kgCO2e/year](https://doi.org/10.17863/CAM.93287), roughly the equivalent of a typical UK home's electricity for a week according to [Ofgem](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/Summary-of-changes-to-energy-price-cap-1-July-to-30-September-2026-revised-TDCV.pdf).
- Take Dr Rob Chisholm's ([Research Software Engineering team](https://rse.shef.ac.uk/)) course on [Performance Profiling & Optimisation](https://rse.shef.ac.uk/pando-python/profiling-introduction.html) for Python.

# Further resources

Here are a few resources to explore more about green computing:

- [SDRA training](https://sdratraining.sparkhub.eu/): A free 30-minute online course by [SPARKHUB](https://sparkhub.eu/) on Sustainable Digital Research Activities for anyone using digital tools in their work.
- [Green RSE SIG](https://socrse.github.io/green-sig/): A national special interest group which aims to build a supportive, collaborative community of research software engineers and researchers who code, to work together on understanding how to reduce the environmental impact of research software.
- [Green DiSC](https://www.software.ac.uk/GreenDiSC): A digital sustainability certification for research groups, computing teams and institutions with guidance, roadmaps and peer-to-peer reviewing to help tackle the environmental impacts of computing activities.
- [Website Carbon calculator](https://www.websitecarbon.com/): Check your website for its carbon footprint.
