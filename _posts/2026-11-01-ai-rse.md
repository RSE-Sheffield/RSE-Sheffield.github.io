## Preamble

The cost of producing software is dropping. Everybody, with the help of a few prompts can produce thousands and thousands of lines of code. This is also the case for words, and the cost of text is also falling dramatically.
As we have recently seen, conference registered abstracts are skyrocketing (ICLR Went From 490 registered abstract to 62,000 in Ten Years[^1]) and we start to see *research papers* made by people claiming openly that they did not and were not able to verify their content[^2].

**As a consequence, where does the value of a research output lie?** 

In the thousands lines of code produced with a mere understanding of what the software does? Or is it coming from the knowledge of the data workflow, the architecture, the implementation of the algorithms, their reusability (in the widest, FAIR[^3], definition of the word), accuracy and impact?<br>
In the thousands of words that constitute a paper? Or from the framing of the problem, the understanding of the process, of the chain of thought, logical steps and the judgement of the results in a wider research context?

<div class="alert alert-primary"> The value lies, and should always lie, on the understanding of a research question, the proposed solution and how this solution has been brought to existence. <b>The currency of research is knowledge, not volume</b>.</div>

AI can be a very powerful ally and can speed up the production of knowledge, but can also have a real pernicious impact on human interactions and collaborations if it is not used in a responsible and collegial way, with mutual understanding and respect. 


**This AI guidance is not about AI-assisted coding**. It is not a list of best practices applied to AI generated code. This guidance is about how, in the RSE team, we value the work we bring to research projects and how we value the work of our research colleagues, their time and their wellbeing, at any stage of their career (PhDs, PDRAs and Researchers). It is about how we want to keep research and technical rigor, human growth and development, and a positive team culture in an epoch where volume seems to be the only metric that matters. 
And finally, this is an attempt to maximise the positive impact of AI in research while trying to minimize the negative aspects.

        <ul>
          <li><b> Human to human interaction is what makes a collaboration.</b>  Exchange of AI information that is then understood only when deciphered by another AI is not a productive way to work. AI to AI is not a proxy for human to human. </li>
          <li><b> AI usage is agreed upon.</b>  When a collaboration starts, the AI usage shall be discussed among the team members and agreed upon.</li>
          <li><b> AI is not a sustainable shortcut.</b> When adding AI-made content on top of AI-made content decreases the overall understanding of the work, AI usage should be reconsidered.</li>
          <li><b> AI transparency.</b> Using AI is perfectly fine, but its usage should be disclosed.</li>
          <li><b> Humans are accountable, not the AI.</b> Validation and quality of work, human or AI made, is a human judgment. Credit for good work goes to humans, consequences for bad work as well. 
                <ul>
                    <li> <span class="text-primary">RSE corollary #2</span>: Software, human or AI made, should follow research standards (e.g. FAIR principles).</li>
                    <li> <span class="text-primary">RSE corollary #1</span>: Software tests, human or AI made, are validated by a human.</li>
                </ul>

          </li>
        
         <li> <b> AI setup is shared. </b> AI content heavily depends on the prompt that generated it. When AI content is shared, the prompt should also be distributed. When a full agentic setup is used, the orchestration specification, model configuration and logs should be freely accessible by the team.  </li>

        </ul>

            <li> <b> Work is human digestible. </b> Contributions made to the team, or exchange of information between team members, are made in reasonable volume. </li>
                <ul>
                    <li> <span class="text-primary">RSE corollary #3</span>: Software development is made by small incremental changes. These changes are recorded in the development history (incremental version control).</li>
                </ul>
    
            <li> <b> Work can be reused by a human. </b> Any type of AI generated output can be understood, modified, built upon, or incorporated into other works without the use of AI. </li>
